Why AstroSphere

The big networks weren’t built for you.

They were built to hold anybody’s attention for as long as possible. A love of space is slow and patient: long nights at the eyepiece, hours of imaging, launches that slip a day and go the next. It was never going to win that fight. Here’s what’s different.

Everyone here loves space

No crossover content. No adverts between the nebulae. No politics, no drama, no influencers. Open the app and it’s wall-to-wall space, every time.

You see the sky you asked for

Your feed isn’t a slot machine. It’s sorted newest first and filtered by you. Tap Planetary and that’s all you see. No mystery ranking.

Your gear is part of the conversation

Every post can carry the telescope and camera that took it. “What did you shoot that with?” is already answered on the post.

Real conversations, properly threaded

Comments nest into replies, and replies into replies, so a back-and-forth about guiding errors, or a detailed collimation conversation, stays readable.

Small rooms, not a stadium

Squads gather your local club, your imaging buddies, or the various other people obsessed with solar prominences, each with a private feed.

You’re in charge of your own peace

Your account can be private. You choose exactly which notifications reach you. Nothing is turned on behind your back.

It isn’t a bigger social network. It’s a smaller, better one, and that’s precisely the point.

Running a Squad

Run it well.

Owners get a manager’s view of their Squad.

  • Approve or decline the people knocking at the door, so private groups stay genuinely private. They’re notified the moment you accept.
  • Appoint moderators to help keep things friendly, and remove anyone who doesn’t belong.
  • Join as many Squads as you like. Your Squad list keeps them one tap from the home screen, and Leave Squad is always one tap away, no awkwardness required.
Crewmates & followers

Two ways to connect, because not every connection means the same thing.

  • Crewmates are your inner circle. A mutual connection you both agreed to. You send a request, they accept. These are the people you’d text at midnight when the clouds finally break.
  • Following is lighter. See someone whose imaging is extraordinary? Follow them and they’re added to your Following list, giving you easy access to their profile and posts. No request, no obligation, no need for them to follow back.
  • Search by name to find friends from your local club, or the imager whose work you keep admiring.
Your profile

Your profile is your observatory.

It’s where the community gets to know you, and it’s genuinely lovely to scroll back through a year of your own posts and see the improvement. That first wobbly Moon shot never stops being a good story.

  • Your name and profile picture. Make it one of your own shots.
  • A bio and your location, so people can find observers near them.
  • A gallery of your best work, your full equipment list, and everything you’ve posted.
  • Private if you want to be. Only people you approve see what you share. Edit your profile or close your account from your own settings. No emails to support, no hoops.

How do I delete my account?

Tap the gear icon on SkyFeed > Delete account (or Edit Profile > Delete account). If you can’t sign in, email support from the address on your account.

How do I make my account private?

Switch your account to private in your profile settings. Only people you approve can then see what you share. You can switch back at any time.

How do I choose which notifications I get?

Open Notifications and tap Settings. Every notification type can be switched on or off on its own.

How do I report a post or user?

Tap the ⋮ on any post, comment or profile and choose Report. You can also block someone from the same menu. Every report is reviewed.

I forgot my password

On the Sign In screen tap “Forgot password?” and we’ll email you a reset link.
