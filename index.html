You finally pull a spiral arm out of the noise. You post it, and your aunt comments “pretty!” while the algorithm buries it under a cat video.

You know the feeling. You stay up until 2am in the freezing cold, you nail the focus, you stack four hours of subs. AstroSphere is where that photo gets the audience it deserves.

Everyone in here knows what seeing conditions are. Nobody needs you to explain why you’re thrilled about a 40-second exposure of a planet. Beginners are welcome too: if you’re wondering whether that fuzzy blob was the Andromeda Galaxy, it probably was. It only gets better from here.

No noise. No strangers. No algorithm deciding your galaxy isn’t engaging enough. Just the sky, and the people who love it.

Why AstroSphere

The big networks weren’t built for you.

They were built to hold anybody’s attention for as long as possible. Astronomy is a slow, patient, quiet hobby, and it was never going to win that fight. Here’s what’s different.

Everyone here is an astronomer

No crossover content. No adverts between the nebulae. No politics, no drama, no influencers. Open the app and it’s wall-to-wall night sky, every time.

You see the sky you asked for

Your feed isn’t a slot machine. It’s sorted newest first and filtered by you. Tap Planetary and that’s all you see. No mystery ranking.

Your gear is part of the conversation

Every post can carry the telescope and camera that took it. “What did you shoot that with?” is already answered on the picture.

Real conversations, properly threaded

Comments nest into replies, and replies into replies, so a back-and-forth about guiding errors stays readable.

Small rooms, not a stadium

Squads gather your local club, your imaging buddies, or the twelve other people obsessed with solar prominences, each with a private feed.

You’re in charge of your own peace

Your account can be private. You choose exactly which notifications reach you. Nothing is turned on behind your back.

It isn’t a bigger social network. It’s a smaller, better one, and that’s precisely the point.

The Sky Feed

Your window on the universe.

The Sky Feed is the heart of AstroSphere. It’s where everything the community captured last night lands. Across the top sit the category filters. One tap and the whole feed narrows.

  • Entire UniverseEverything, from everyone
  • LunarThe Moon in all her phases, craters and terminator detail
  • SolarSunspots, prominences, white light and hydrogen-alpha
  • ISSSpace station passes, trails and transits
  • RocketsLaunches, launch trails and the hardware behind them
  • PlanetaryJupiter’s belts, Saturn’s rings, the Martian polar caps
  • Deep SkyGalaxies, nebulae, clusters, everything faint and far away
  • MiscellaneousAurorae, meteors, kit shots, nightscapes and happy accidents
Share what you caught

From the back garden to the Sky Feed in three steps.

Tap the +Post button and you’re away. Write as much or as little as you like, add a single photo or a whole set from one session, and pick the category it belongs in. Change your mind later? Every post can be edited or deleted. No typo lives forever.

Likes & comments

Talk about it properly.

Give a post a like, and tap the count to see everyone else who did. Then join the conversation.

  • Likes on comments too. A genuinely helpful answer rises to the top where the next person will find it.

Running a Squad

Run it well.

Owners get a manager’s view of their Squad.

  • Approve or decline the people knocking at the door, so private groups stay genuinely private. They’re notified the moment you accept.
  • Appoint moderators to help keep things friendly, and remove anyone who doesn’t belong.
  • Join as many Squads as you like. Your Squad list keeps them one tap from the home screen, and Leave Squad is always one tap away, no awkwardness required.
Crewmates & followers

Two ways to connect, because not every connection means the same thing.

  • Crewmates are your inner circle. A mutual connection you both agreed to. You send a request, they accept. These are the people you’d text at midnight when the clouds finally break.
  • Following is lighter. See someone whose imaging is extraordinary? Follow them and their posts appear in your feed. No request, no obligation, no need for them to follow back.
  • Search by name to find friends from your local club, or the imager whose work you keep admiring.
Your profile

Your profile is your observatory.

It’s where the community gets to know you, and it’s genuinely lovely to scroll back through a year of your own posts and see the improvement. That first wobbly Moon shot never stops being a good story.

  • Your name, photo and a banner behind it. Make it one of your own shots.
  • A bio and your location, so people can find observers near them.
  • A gallery of your best work, your full equipment list, and everything you’ve posted.
  • Private if you want to be. Only people you approve see what you share. Edit your profile or close your account from your own settings. No emails to support, no hoops.
A daily dose of wonder

NASA’s Astronomy Picture of the Day, built right in.

Every day NASA publishes an extraordinary image of the cosmos with an explanation written by professional astronomers. AstroSphere brings it to the top of your feed: the picture, the title and the story behind it.

It’s a free astronomy lesson every morning. Some days it’s a Hubble masterpiece, some days it’s an amateur’s shot that stopped NASA in their tracks.

Notifications

Notifications that respect you.

AstroSphere tells you when something genuinely involving you happens. Then comes the part the big platforms never offer: you choose which of those reach you.

  • Someone liked your post, commented, or replied to your comment
  • Someone started following you, or wants to be your crewmate
  • Your Squad request was accepted, or politely declined

Every type can be switched off individually. An unread badge keeps count, and one tap on Mark all seen clears the decks when you come back from holiday.

Who it’s for

You don’t need to own a telescope to belong here.

If you’re brand new

This is the friendliest place to start. Ask the questions you’re embarrassed to ask, and see what people with your budget are managing. Post that first slightly blurry Moon shot. Everyone here has one.

If you’re an astrophotographer

Your work is understood here. Tag your rig, compare notes on the same targets, and build a gallery that shows years of getting better at a very hard thing.

If you observe visually

No camera required. Describe what you saw, share a sketch, talk about the night the Veil Nebula finally appeared.

If you’re in a club or society

Make it a Squad. Post session reports, organise dark-sky trips and keep the group talking between meetings, all in one private space.

If you’re just curious about space

Follow people whose work amazes you, and let NASA’s picture of the day quietly make your mornings better.

Clear skies. We’ll see you up there.

Astronomy is the oldest science there is, and the one that still makes grown adults gasp out loud in a cold field at 2am. It deserves better than being an afterthought on somebody else’s platform. So here’s AstroSphere: built for one community, run at the pace of the night sky.

How do I delete my account?

Open My Page, choose Edit Profile, then Delete account. Your profile, posts, comments and equipment list are removed. If you can’t sign in, email us from the address on your account.

How do I make my account private?

Switch your account to private in your profile settings. Only people you approve can then see what you share. You can switch back at any time.

How do I choose which notifications I get?

Open Notifications and tap Settings. Every notification type can be switched on or off on its own.

How do I report a post or user?

Email us with the username and a description of the post. We review every report.

I forgot my password

On the Sign In screen tap “Forgot password?” and we’ll email you a reset link.
